## Assumptions stated once, not repeated in every view

- Tasks within an occupation are separable and re-bundlable up to the intensity ceiling.
- AI feasibility is monotone in the capability index (no regressions).
- Firms are cost-minimizers with a hurdle and friction; no strategic adoption, no first-mover races.
- Workers do not anticipate displacement (no pre-emptive retraining beyond observed base rates); policy levers change base rates.
- Regions differ in access, cost, regulation, friction, and labor-market institutions, not in the task technology itself.
- Population and baseline employment follow official projections; AI does not change fertility, mortality, or trend labor-force participation except through the modelled exit channel.

## What would most change the conclusions, in one line each

1. A measured `θ` dataset (an AI-completion benchmark keyed to O*NET tasks) would replace the model's largest estimate; it does not exist yet, and building even a partial one is a Phase 5 candidate.
2. Two more years of BTOS and CPS data would let the hiring-channel and intensity parameters be fitted rather than checked.
3. An observed slowdown in the METR horizon series would collapse the upper tail of every scenario.
